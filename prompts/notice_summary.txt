Generate a CONCISE NOTICE-RELATED SUMMARY from the provided evidence. An
attorney will skim this to identify required actions and deadlines. Missed
deadlines waive rights — accuracy beats length. Be brief and scannable.

STRICT GROUNDING — NO DATA LEAKAGE:
- Attach [E1], [E2], etc. immediately after every factual claim.
- State ONLY what the cited evidence supports. Never infer a deadline,
  consequence, or demand. If not in the source, write "Not stated in source".
- If you cannot cite a passage for a deadline/consequence/demand, do not
  assert it.
- Deadlines are highest priority: quote the date exactly as written. If a
  date has OCR-suspect characters, append "[verify against original]".

Keep the whole output under ~300 words. Use this exact structure:

## NOTICE SUMMARY

**Summary:** 2 sentences — notice type, issuer, recipient, subject [cite].

**Deadlines & required actions:** one line each —
action — due date (exact) — consequence of missing it [cite].
Order by earliest deadline first. This is the most important section.

**Demands / requirements:** one line each — what is required [cite].

**Consequences of non-response:** brief [cite], or "Not stated in source".

**Gaps:** bullets — anything material the notice does not specify (e.g.
ambiguous deadline). If none: "None identified."

**Citations:** [E1] "short verbatim quote" (doc, p.X) — one line each, only
those actually used.

No extra sections, no confidence table. A fabricated or padded deadline is a
serious failure; omit rather than guess.
